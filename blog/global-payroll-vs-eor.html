Global Payroll vs. EOR: Which International Hiring Strategy is Right for You?

David Chen August 6, 2026 9 min read

As companies scale their global footprints, HR and finance leaders must decide how to pay their international teams. Two primary options dominate the market: Global Payroll and Employer of Record (EOR). While they sound similar, they represent entirely different operational and legal strategies.

Selecting the wrong path can lead to severe compliance issues, high overhead, and unhappy employees. In this comparison guide, we will examine the structural differences between Global Payroll and EOR to help you choose the right model for your business objectives.

The Core Difference: Legal Entity Ownership

The defining difference between Global Payroll and EOR is who owns the legal entity employing the worker.

  • Employer of Record (EOR): The EOR provider owns the legal entity in the destination country and acts as the legal employer. You do not need a business registry in that country.
  • Global Payroll: Your company owns the legal entity in the destination country. You register as an employer, set up local bank accounts, and use a global payroll platform to calculate taxes, process payments, and file local filings.

When to Choose an EOR Model

The EOR model is best suited for companies that:

  • Are testing a new international market and do not want to commit to setting up a local office.
  • Need to onboard and pay employees in a new country within days.
  • Only plan to hire a small number of employees (e.g., 1 to 5) in a specific country.
  • Want to offload all compliance liability, benefits administration, and tax calculations.

"Using an EOR is the quickest way to hire globally. It is an operational shortcut that delivers immediate legal safety."

When to Transition to Global Payroll

A transition to owning an entity and utilizing Global Payroll is typically recommended when:

  • You have a large team (typically 10-15+ employees) in a single country. At this scale, EOR monthly fees can exceed the cost of maintaining a legal entity.
  • You require a permanent physical presence (offices, manufacturing, local retail) in the country.
  • You wish to establish a strong, localized employer brand and directly manage long-term employee relations.
