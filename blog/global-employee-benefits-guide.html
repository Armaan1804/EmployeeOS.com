Global Benefits Guide: Competitive Packages for Remote Teams

Chloe Dupont July 14, 2026 9 min read

Attracting top international talent requires more than offering a competitive base salary. Today's remote workers expect comprehensive benefits packages that support their health, family, and professional growth. However, local regulations vary dramatically, making global benefits administration a complex task.

In this guide, we outline how to structure a competitive, compliant, and localized global employee benefits package for your distributed team.

1. Understand Statutory (Mandatory) Benefits First

Before offering supplementary perks, you must meet the mandatory legal requirements of the employee's country. Failing to provide statutory benefits constitutes a serious compliance violation.

Examples of statutory benefits include:

  • 13th and 14th Month Salaries: Mandatory bonus payments in countries like Italy, Brazil, and the Philippines.
  • Statutory Pension Contributions: Required employer pension payments, such as Superannuation in Australia or CPF in Singapore.
  • Mandatory Paid Time Off (PTO): While the US has no federal mandatory PTO, European countries typically require a minimum of 20 to 25 days of paid annual leave.

"A competitive global benefits strategy must cover mandatory requirements while offering supplementary perks that match local market expectations."

2. Supplementary Benefits that Stand Out

To win top talent from large tech giants, you should offer supplementary benefits customized for each region:

  • Private Health Insurance: In countries with public healthcare (like the UK or Canada), supplementary private health insurance provides faster care and dental/vision benefits. In the US, comprehensive health plans are essential.
  • Home Office Stipends: A one-off budget (e.g., $500 to $1,000) for ergonomic chairs, monitors, and reliable keyboards, helping remote employees work comfortably and productively.
  • Learning and Development (L&D): Annual learning stipends to cover online courses, technical books, and industry conference tickets.

3. Localize, Do Not Standardize

A common mistake is offering a standardized benefit package designed for headquarters to all global employees. For instance, offering a US 401(k) retirement plan to a software developer in France is useless and legally impossible.
