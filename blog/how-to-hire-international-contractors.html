How to Compliantly Hire and Pay International Contractors

Marcus Aureli August 3, 2026 8 min read

Hiring international contractors is one of the fastest ways to scale your engineering, creative, or operations teams. It offers flexibility, lower administrative overhead, and access to unique skill sets. However, working with global freelancers requires careful attention to compliance to avoid severe legal complications.

In this guide, we will walk you through the essential compliance checks, documentation, and payment best practices required when engaging international contractors.

1. Establish a Written, Locally Compliant Agreement

A handshake agreement or a generic contract template is not sufficient for global contractors. Your contractor agreement must be tailored to the contractor's local jurisdiction to ensure validity.

Key elements of a robust international contractor agreement include:

  • Statement of Work (SOW): Detailed scope of projects, deadlines, and deliverables. This reinforces that the worker is hired for a specific result, not ongoing open-ended services.
  • Independent Status Clause: Expressly stating that the relationship is that of an independent business, not an employee.
  • IP & Confidentiality: Clear terms specifying that all intellectual property transfers to your company immediately upon payment.
  • Termination Terms: Notice periods and conditions for ending the contract, aligned with local commercial codes.

"Without a clear, locally compliant contract, local courts may default to classifying your contractor as an employee, exposing your company to severe liabilities."

2. Collect Necessary Tax and Identity Documentation

If your company is based in the United States, you must collect specific tax forms to report contractor payments and comply with IRS guidelines:

  • Form W-8BEN (for individuals) or W-8BEN-E (for entities): Confirms the contractor's foreign status and certifies that the work is performed outside the US, meaning no US tax withholding is required.
  • Form W-9: If the worker is a US citizen living abroad, they must submit a W-9, and you may need to file a 1099-NEC.
  • Certificate of Incorporation: Request this if the contractor operates through their own local corporate entity.

3. Secure Your Intellectual Property (IP)

IP laws vary wildly between countries. In some jurisdictions, IP generated by a contractor does not automatically transfer to the hiring company unless a specific local legal formula is used. Ensure your agreements explicitly cover international IP transfers according to local rules.

4. Pay Contractors in Their Preferred Currency

Sending USD wire transfers directly to international banks often incurs high wire transfer fees (ranging from $30 to $50 per transaction) and unfavorable exchange rates. To keep contractors happy and keep your costs low:

  • Use global payment platforms that support local bank transfers (like ACH, SEPA, or Pix).
  • Allow contractors to invoice and receive payments in their local currency.
  • Consolidate contractor payments to pay all invoices globally in one batch click.
