How to Establish a Foreign Entity (And Why EOR is Faster)

David Chen July 9, 2026 10 min read

When expanding business operations abroad, executive teams face a major strategic decision: should you establish a local subsidiary (foreign entity setup) or use an Employer of Record (EOR) to hire local workers?

This decision impacts your expansion timeline, corporate compliance risks, capital requirements, and HR operations. In this guide, we provide a detailed cost-benefit analysis of foreign entity setup versus EOR.

The Foreign Entity Setup Process

Establishing a legal subsidiary in a new country is a major legal and administrative project. It requires:

  • Local Registration: Filing articles of incorporation and registering with local tax and business authorities.
  • Local Directors: Many countries require a local citizen to act as a resident director, which often requires hiring a specialized director service.
  • Capital Requirements: Depositing minimum share capital (which can range from $5,000 to $50,000+) in a local bank account.
  • Ongoing Maintenance: Filing corporate tax returns, managing local payroll, maintaining offices, and hiring local accountants.

"Setting up an entity requires significant initial capital and a long-term commitment. EOR services let you bypass this completely."

Why Companies Choose EOR for International Expansion

An Employer of Record (EOR) is a legal shortcut that bypasses subsidiary setup entirely. It provides key advantages:

  • Speed: Go live and hire employees in a new market in as little as 48 hours, compared to months for entity registration.
  • Low Risk: If a market doesn't perform well, you can close operations and offboard employees easily. Closing a corporate legal entity is highly complex and costly.
  • Outsourced Liability: The EOR provider assumes all legal risks associated with local labor compliance, payroll taxes, and audits.
